Graphene

Graphene is a variety of the element carbon which occurs naturally in small amounts. In graphene, the carbon forms a sheet of interlocked atoms as hexagons one carbon atom thick.

Graphene is known for its exceptionally high tensile strength, electrical conductivity, transparency, and being the thinnest two-dimensional material in the world. 1On a microscopic scale, graphene is the strongest material ever measured, with a tensile strength of 2130 GPa and a Young's modulus of about 1 TPa.

The material was first isolated and characterized in 2004 by Andre Geim and Konstantin Novoselov at the University of Manchester.

Rules a reviewer can check

  • Every hairline outside the Arc base joins two things that came from each other, or sits at the left of a quote. Point at any line and name both ends.
  • Serif appears only on text that came from a page. Never on a heading, label or control.
  • The lattice appears only on empty Threads, empty Vault, the empty new-tab page and the Board canvas.
  • Every identity element disappears when its data is gone. An empty space is pixel-identical to Arc, except graphite.
  • The accent is the only colour Graphene owns; semantic red is for errors and unread only.
