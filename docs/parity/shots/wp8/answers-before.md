## Graphene (Wikipedia)

Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphene

Question: What is graphene, and how does it differ from graphite?

Stream updates: 16

Graphene is a single layer of carbon atoms arranged in a hexagonal honeycomb pattern. It is the thinnest two-dimensional material in the world and is known for its exceptional strength, electrical conductivity, and transparency. Graphene is a carbon allotrope, meaning it is a different form of carbon with distinct properties.

Graphite, on the other hand, is a three-dimensional form of carbon composed of many layers of graphene stacked together. These layers are held together by weak van der Waals forces. Because of this structure, graphite appears black and absorbs all visible light wavelengths.

In summary, the primary difference between graphene and graphite is their structure: graphene is a single layer of carbon atoms, while graphite is composed of many layers of graphene stacked together.
